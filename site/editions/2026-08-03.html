Blanche Formally Withdraws Trump 'Anti-Weaponization' Fund Plan

Todd Blanche formally withdrew the Trump administration's 'anti-weaponization' fund proposal after two Republican senators demanded a written guarantee the plan would not be revived. The senators withheld support until receiving that commitment in writing. The withdrawal marks a rare instance of Republican lawmakers extracting a formal concession from the administration on a contested funding mechanism.

Why it matters: The episode shows Senate Republicans are willing to use their leverage to block White House proposals they find constitutionally or politically problematic.

4 Million Fewer Americans Now Receive SNAP Food Stamps

SNAP participation has dropped sharply and consistently since last July, leaving more than 4 million people without food aid. The decline coincides with historic cuts and structural changes to the program, commonly known as food stamps. The trend represents one of the steepest participation drops the program has seen, unfolding rapidly as federal policy shifts continue to reshape who qualifies and receives benefits.

Why it matters: If you or someone you know relies on SNAP, ongoing program changes mean eligibility and benefit levels may no longer be what they were even a year ago.

Sources: Over 4 million people are no longer receiving food aid amid sweeping changes to SNAP
Business / Policy

Capital One Closed Trump Accounts Over Money-Laundering Review, Not Jan. 6

Capital One told a judge it shut down hundreds of Trump Organization accounts in 2021 following a monthslong internal money-laundering review, not as a reaction to the January 6 Capitol riot. The bank made the argument in a motion seeking permanent dismissal of a lawsuit filed by Trump-affiliated businesses, which had challenged the account closures.

Why it matters: The case tests whether banks can close high-profile accounts on compliance grounds without facing litigation over political motivation claims.

Sources: Capital One says it closed Trump Organization accounts over money-laundering concerns
Science / Tech / Health

Veterinarians Begin Making Hive Calls as Honeybee Decline Deepens

Veterinarians are increasingly taking on honeybee patients as the vital agricultural pollinators face growing threats. The shift marks a significant expansion of veterinary practice into apiary health, with practitioners making on-site hive visits to assess and treat colonies. Honeybees play a critical role in food production, pollinating a wide range of crops. The move is described as cautious, with the veterinary profession gingerly embracing bees as a new area of care.

Why it matters: Healthier honeybee colonies mean more reliable pollination of the fruits, vegetables, and nuts that stock grocery store shelves and determine food prices.

Sources: Meet the Bee Vet, Now Making Hive Calls
Education

440 Private Colleges At Risk as Campus Closings Accelerate

More than 440 private nonprofit four-year colleges and universities nationwide are now at risk of closing, driven by falling enrollment, rising debt, and related financial pressures. The pace of closings is accelerating, producing a growing market in abandoned campuses. Burlington College in Vermont, which closed in 2016, is among past examples. The trend reflects deepening structural strain across American higher education.

Why it matters: Families choosing a private college today face a real risk that the institution may not survive long enough to deliver the degree — leaving students with debt and no diploma.

Sources: Pace of college closings picks up, with more projected
Personal Excellence

AI App Accelerates Criminal Record Expungement for Millions of Eligible Americans

Tens of millions of Americans carry criminal records that block access to employment. A new AI-powered app is helping eligible individuals expunge those records at a pace not previously seen. The tool targets a longstanding bottleneck in the expungement process, which has historically moved slowly due to legal complexity and limited access to attorneys, leaving many qualified people unable to clear their records despite meeting legal criteria.

Why it matters: For the millions of Americans whose criminal records limit job prospects, faster expungement access could mean a direct path back to stable employment and financial footing.

Sources: This lawyer believes in second chances. Now AI is providing a boost

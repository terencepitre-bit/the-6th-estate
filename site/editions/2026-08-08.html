Russian Missiles Kill Three Near Kyiv, Including a Child

Russia launched overnight missile attacks near Kyiv, killing three people, including a child. The strikes came as President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Ukraine's supply of missile interceptors is dwindling. The timing underscores the growing danger posed by depleted air-defense resources as Russia maintains its bombardment campaign against Ukrainian civilian areas.

Why it matters: Ukraine's shrinking stockpile of missile interceptors means more incoming strikes are likely to reach populated areas, putting civilian lives at direct and immediate risk.

Money & Markets

Trump Renews Push to Fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook Over Fraud Claims

President Trump is again threatening to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, citing unproven allegations of mortgage fraud. An earlier attempt to oust Cook was struck down by the Supreme Court. Cook, appointed by President Biden, holds a seat on the Fed's Board of Governors, which sets U.S. monetary policy including interest rates.

Why it matters: A successful removal of a sitting Fed governor would shake the central bank's independence and could directly affect the interest rates consumers pay on mortgages, car loans, and credit cards.

Sources: Trump renews push to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook
Business / Policy

Judge Orders Meta to Fund $567M Youth Mental Health Program

A New Mexico judge ruled that Meta caused a 'public nuisance' and ordered the company to establish a $567 million fund to address the youth mental health crisis. The ruling marks a significant legal finding against the social media giant, holding it financially responsible for harms linked to its platforms and requiring it to pay into treatment and support programs for young people.

Why it matters: The $567 million fund could direct real money toward mental health treatment for children and teenagers in New Mexico, with implications for how courts hold social media companies accountable nationwide.

Sources: Judge rules Meta caused "public nuisance" and must fund mental health treatment
Science / Tech / Health

Copernicus Browser Adds Wildfire Tracking During Record Fire Season

The Copernicus Browser, Europe's free satellite service, added a wildfire visualization feature during what has been described as a record wildfire season. The new tool makes it easier to track active fires and their spread using satellite data the platform already provides at no cost to users.

Why it matters: Free, real-time wildfire tracking gives residents, emergency planners, and local officials faster access to fire location data that can inform evacuation decisions and resource deployment.

Sources: Europe's free satellite service just made it easier to track wildfires
Education

Head Start Providers Blast Federal Proposal to Strip Program's Core Services

Head Start providers are pushing back against a federal proposal they say would fundamentally dismantle the 60-year-old preschool program. Providers warn the changes would eliminate wraparound services — including health screenings and family support — that distinguish Head Start from standard childcare. One Washington state parent credited a Head Start teacher's early observation with connecting her son to speech therapy. Critics say the proposal would reduce the program to basic childcare in name only.

Why it matters: Families with young children enrolled in Head Start — nearly 800,000 nationally — stand to lose health, developmental, and social services that the program currently delivers alongside preschool education.

Sources: ‘It wouldn’t be Head Start anymore:’ Providers blast federal proposal to alter preschool program
Personal Excellence

India Moves to Recover 'Green Boots,' Frozen on Everest 30 Years

An Indian climber, known for decades as 'Green Boots' for his distinctive lime-colored footwear, has served as a grim trail marker on Mount Everest since his death roughly 30 years ago. His body, visible to climbers navigating the northeast ridge route, became one of the mountain's most recognized landmarks. India is now moving to finally recover his remains and bring them home.

Why it matters: The case renews debate over how nations and mountaineering authorities handle the estimated 200-plus bodies still frozen on Everest, raising questions about dignity, recovery costs, and who bears responsibility.

Sources: He's been frozen on Everest for 30 years, now 'Green Boots' may finally be coming home

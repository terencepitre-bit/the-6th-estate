Missile Leaves 10-Meter Crater Inside Poland Near Ukraine Border

A missile that Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk described as probably Russian struck near the village of Tarnawa Kolonia, roughly 100 kilometers from the Ukrainian border, leaving a crater 10 meters wide. The strike occurred during a Russian attack on the Ukrainian city of Lviv. The object's origin remains under investigation by Polish authorities.

Why it matters: A strike on Polish territory, a NATO member state, raises the specter of alliance-wide military obligations under Article 5 — with direct consequences for U.S. foreign policy and the broader course of the war in Ukraine.

Science / Tech / Health

Congress Unites Against Pentagon's Stalled PFAS Cleanup at Hundreds of Bases

Bipartisan anger is growing in Congress over the Pentagon's delayed cleanup of PFAS contamination at hundreds of military bases across the country. Lawmakers from both parties are demanding explanations for the slow pace of remediation. PFAS, known as forever chemicals, are linked to low birth weight and other health risks. Politicians have not yet received satisfactory answers from the Defense Department on timelines or accountability.

Why it matters: Families living near contaminated military bases face ongoing exposure to chemicals linked to low birth weight and other serious health risks until cleanups are completed.

Sources: Bipartisan Anger Grows at Pentagon’s Delayed ‘Forever Chemicals’ Cleanups

98 Teens Draft Their Own AI Rules for Schools After Congress Stalls

With no federal AI legislation for schools in place, roughly 98 teenagers convened to write their own policy framework addressing student use of AI on assignments and tests, and who should be responsible for teaching AI literacy. The student-led effort fills a vacuum left by Congress, which has not passed rules governing how artificial intelligence tools are used in K-12 education.

Why it matters: The policies governing AI in your children's classrooms are being shaped without federal guidance, leaving schools — and families — to navigate the rules largely on their own.

Personal Excellence

Temple University's Inez Patterson Defied Segregated Pool Hours in 1920s Philadelphia

Nearly 100 years ago, Inez Patterson integrated swimming pools in Philadelphia by refusing to accept segregated swim hours at Temple University. When her white teammates swam, Patterson insisted on swimming alongside them rather than being restricted to the limited hours designated for Black swimmers. She also encouraged other Black girls to swim, expanding her individual act of defiance into a broader push for inclusion in aquatic spaces.

Why it matters: Patterson's resistance to pool segregation at Temple is an early chapter in the long fight for equal access to public recreational spaces that Black Americans continued to wage for decades after.

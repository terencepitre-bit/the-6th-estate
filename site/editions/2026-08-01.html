Death Toll Hits 67 as Thousands Storm Spain's Ceuta Border

At least 67 people died in Spain's Ceuta border crisis with Morocco, including migrants who drowned and others killed in a stampede to cross a breakwater barrier. Tens of thousands have since crossed back into Morocco following the mass border event at the Spanish enclave on the northern African coast.

Why it matters: The crisis marks one of the deadliest border incidents in recent European history, intensifying pressure on Spain and the EU over migration policy and border security.

Fed Proposes Modernized Limits on Loans to Bank Executives and Insiders

The Federal Reserve Board opened a public comment period on a proposal to update its rules governing credit extended to bank insiders — a category that includes executives, board members, and major shareholders with potential influence over a bank's lending decisions. The existing rule, which sets conditions and limits on such loans, would be modernized under the proposal. The Fed is soliciting feedback before finalizing any changes.

Why it matters: Updated insider lending rules could affect how banks manage conflicts of interest at the top, with direct implications for the safety of deposits held by everyday customers.

Sources: Federal Reserve Board requests comment on a proposal to modernize its rule governing the extension of credit to bank "insiders"—bank executives, board members and major shareholders who could potentially influence a bank's lending decisions · Federal Reserve Board requests comment on a proposal to modernize rules for mutual banking organizations

Trump's Media Company Sells Early Access to His Posts for $100K a Month

Trump's media company is offering investors early access to the president's Truth Social posts for fees reaching $100,000 a month. Legal experts say the paid subscription service may violate insider trading laws, as Trump's posts frequently move markets and influence policy expectations. The tiered early-access program gives paying subscribers a window to act on the president's statements before they reach the general public.

Why it matters: If the service violates insider trading laws, ordinary investors who lack $100,000-a-month access could be systematically disadvantaged every time the president posts market-moving content.

Trump Administration Rolls Back Biden-Era Workplace Heat Protection Rules

The Trump administration is reversing steps taken by the Biden administration to protect workers from extreme heat on the job. Where Biden pursued formal safeguards against dangerous temperatures in the workplace, the Trump administration is adopting a more lenient posture toward employers. The policy shift leaves workers with fewer regulatory protections as heat-related workplace hazards grow.

Why it matters: Workers in physically demanding or outdoor jobs face greater risk of heat illness with weaker federal protections in place.

Pennsylvania High School Stayed Silent After Boys Made AI Nudes of 59 Classmates

A Pennsylvania high school is defending its decision to stay silent after male students used AI to generate nude images of 59 female classmates. The school argues gaps in existing laws limit its obligation to act or notify victims. Why it matters: If your child's school faces a similar incident, existing laws may give administrators legal cover to say nothing — leaving victims and parents in the dark.

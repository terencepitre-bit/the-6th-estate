AI Tokens Emerge as a Key Economic Metric for Tracking AI Adoption

Economists are beginning to use AI tokens — the units AI companies already use to measure usage and bill customers — as a tool to track how broadly AI is spreading through the economy. Much like the kilowatt-hour became a standard measure of electrical consumption, tokens could serve as a common unit for gauging AI activity across industries, giving researchers and policymakers a clearer picture of adoption trends.

Why it matters: A standardized way to measure AI usage could help workers, businesses, and governments better understand how quickly AI is reshaping industries and where its economic impact is being felt most.

Sources: AI tokens could become the kilowatt-hour of the AI age

Ecuador's Police Battle Surging Cocaine Trade on Route to Europe

Ecuador has become a major transit point for cocaine moving from South America to Europe, with police facing a significant rise in drug-related crime. The BBC accompanied Ecuadorian officers on patrol as they attempt to counter the growing power of drug gangs operating through the country. Authorities are under mounting pressure as trafficking networks expand their reach along what has emerged as a key smuggling corridor.

Why it matters: The expansion of cocaine trafficking routes through Ecuador could drive up drug availability and gang violence in destination countries across Europe, affecting public safety and law enforcement priorities far beyond South America.

Southern Oregon University Fights for Survival as Closure Threat Looms

Southern Oregon University in Ashland faces a genuine risk of closure, a scenario once considered unthinkable for a public campus. The university has deep ties to its surrounding community, including outreach programs that have connected local students to music and other academic pursuits for years. Declining enrollment and financial strain have pushed the institution to a critical point, forcing students, faculty, and local residents to mobilize in an effort to keep it open.

Why it matters: If a public university can close, residents in rural and small-city regions could lose affordable, nearby access to higher education, forcing students to relocate or forgo degrees entirely.

Sources: Could a public campus really close? The fight to save Southern Oregon University

Colorado Mountain Town Bets on Crafts Hub to Foster Year-Round Community

A Colorado mountain tourist town is investing in a dedicated building for craftspeople, paired with affordable housing, in an effort to build a more stable year-round community. The initiative aims to move beyond seasonal tourism dependence by giving local artisans a permanent base to live and work. The project reflects a broader challenge facing resort towns where housing costs often push workers and creatives out.

Why it matters: Combining workspace with affordable housing could offer a replicable model for other tourist-dependent towns struggling to retain residents and sustain local economies beyond peak seasons.

Sources: Colorado tourist town invests in crafts to build community

Von der Leyen Demands Stronger EU Borders After Ceuta Migrant Surge

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for action on border security ahead of an urgent EU interior ministers meeting convened to address migrant crossings at Ceuta. The emergency gathering signals mounting pressure on EU member states to coordinate a response to the situation at the Spanish enclave on the northern coast of Africa, a flashpoint for migration into Europe.

Why it matters: Decisions made at this emergency ministers meeting could reshape EU border and migration policy in ways that affect asylum seekers, member states' obligations, and the broader debate over Europe's external frontiers.

SNAP Rolls Shed 4 Million Recipients as Cuts Reshape Food Aid

More than 4 million people have stopped receiving SNAP benefits since last July, as the federal food-stamp program undergoes sweeping cuts and structural changes. The decline has been rapid and consistent across that period. SNAP, which serves as the primary federal nutrition safety net for low-income Americans, is facing what officials and advocates describe as historic reductions in both eligibility and funding.

Why it matters: Millions of households that previously relied on SNAP to cover grocery costs are now navigating that expense without federal assistance.

Science / Tech / Health

Trump Opens Pacific Monument Waters to Commercial Fishing

The Trump administration is lifting protections on U.S. marine monuments in the Pacific Ocean, opening their waters to commercial fishing. The affected areas, centered on remote island chains, are among the largest conservation zones in the country and host some of the most biodiverse and pristine ocean ecosystems in the United States. The move has triggered a dispute between conservation advocates and those supporting expanded fishing access.

Why it matters: Weakened monument protections could accelerate damage to reef ecosystems and fish populations that once recovered behind federal boundaries — affecting seafood supplies and ocean health for generations.

440 Private Colleges At Risk as Campus Closings Accelerate

More than 440 private nonprofit four-year colleges and universities nationwide are now at risk of closing, driven by falling enrollment, rising debt, and related financial pressures. The pace of closings is accelerating, producing a growing market in abandoned campuses. Burlington College in Vermont, which closed in 2016, stands as an early example of the trend now spreading more broadly across American higher education.

Why it matters: If your family is weighing a private college enrollment decision, the financial stability of the institution is now a real variable — students caught in a sudden closure can lose credits, financial aid, and academic momentum.

Sources: Pace of college closings picks up, with more projected

Spokane Wildfires Destroy Hundreds of Homes, Force 60,000 to Evacuate

Wildfires burning in Spokane have destroyed hundreds of homes and triggered evacuation orders for 60,000 people. The fires have swept through residential areas, displacing tens of thousands of residents as emergency crews work to contain the blazes.

Why it matters: If you or someone you know lives in or near Spokane, evacuation orders remain active — follow local emergency alerts and have a go-bag ready.
